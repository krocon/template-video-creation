Ping! An email from your bank: Your account will be locked, unless you click right here.
Wait a second. This is exactly what phishing looks like. Scammers want your credentials.
But you can spot these fake emails by five telltale warning signs:
First: The sender. Hover your cursor over the sender name without clicking. Is it an unfamiliar address? Red flag!
Second: Generic greetings like “Dear Customer”, frequently packed with typos.
Third: Artificial urgency and threats. They want you panicking so you click without thinking.
Fourth: Asking for your password, PIN, or two-factor code. Your real bank will never ask for this by email!
Fifth: Deceptive links and attachments. Always verify where a link leads before clicking, and never open unexpected attachments.
Still unsure? Do not click and do not reply! Reach out to the sender via an official channel, and report the message to your IT security team.
Remember: Pause before you click, don't take the bait!
